# Echo material — Lesson 4.1

*Authored 2026-08-11. This is the MATERIAL for the echo: what the folio
should PLAY, and the copy that goes with it. It is not the engine and it
is not a storage schema — the engine and its schema are `briefs/echo.md`,
written by another hand. Everything here is in neutral notation and is
meant to be encoded into whatever that brief lands on.*

*Machine-readable twin: `quests/echo-material.json` — 27 candidate calls
across 7 rungs, with the notation self-described in its `notation` field.
Nothing in this document contradicts that file; if it ever does, the JSON
is the material and this is the reasoning.*

## What 4.1 is for

`CURRICULUM.md`, Lesson 4: the folio plays a short *hidden* fragment and
the player answers it back on the grid. Hit or miss is grid-checkable, so
the drill corrects without explaining. 4.1 uses folio-generated calls
only — no recordings, which is 4.2's job.

The craft target, in the curriculum's words: **audiation** — holding a
line in the head long enough to find it with the hands — and, under it,
the thing the ladder is actually built to make happen: **contour narrows
a note to two or three candidates before the ear has to decide
anything.** The ladder ends the moment that has landed. It does not try
to make the player good at dictation; it tries to make him notice that
dictation has two halves and that the cheap half comes first.

## Sizing

Seven rungs, one board's worth. This is deliberately the size of an L2
board, not an L3 one — the L3 ruling on record is that three boards and
seventeen quests ran too long and it cost the player his appetite. Three
rungs of contour, two of degrees, one of rhythm, one closer. If the idea
has landed by rung 5, rungs 6 and 7 are still worth playing but the
lesson is already paid for.

Rungs 1–5 are short calls. Only the last two use the full sixteen steps.

## The ladder at a glance

| # | Rung | Stage | Call | Seed |
|---|------|-------|------|------|
| 1 | which way | contour | 2 notes, a fifth or an octave apart | C major, 88 |
| 2 | three ways | contour | 3 notes, fourths and wider, "again" enters | G major, 92 |
| 3 | the long way | contour | 5–6 notes, thirds | D minor, 96 |
| 4 | home and back | degrees | 3 notes, pentatonic only | F major, 96 |
| 5 | the pull | degrees | 4 notes, the semitone step enters | C major, 92 |
| 6 | one note drum | rhythm | 16 steps, one pitch, holds and gaps | A minor, 100 |
| 7 | the sentence | degrees + rhythm | 16 steps, both at once | G major, 92 |

Never three problems at once: contour is asked alone, then pitch alone
(lengths equal), then rhythm alone (pitch fixed). The closer asks two,
and only two, because degrees already contain contour — answering the
pitches answers the shape for free. That is the discovery, arriving as a
convenience rather than as a sentence.

## Design notes per stage

**Contour (1–3).** Intervals are wide on purpose at rung 1 — a fifth or
an octave, four steps each, so the first note is still in the head when
the second arrives. They narrow to thirds by rung 3, never to a step:
by then the difficulty should be the *length* of the call, not the size
of the move. "Again" enters at rung 2 and is a move like any other; the
ear that hears two notes where there were three has already failed the
rung. Only three characters exist in an answer: up, down, again.

**Degrees (4–5).** Rung 4 stays inside 1 2 3 5 6 — the pentatonic set
the L1–L3 pages already lean on, no semitone anywhere in it, nothing that
pulls. Rung 5 takes the fence down: every call touches the scale step
sitting a semitone under the tonic (or its neighbour above the fifth),
and that step behaves differently from the others. Major keys at rung 5
on purpose — the natural minor the instrument writes has no such step,
and 4.1 spends no chromatics at all. Lengths are equal throughout both
rungs so rhythm is not smuggled in as a second question.

**Rhythm (6).** One pitch, sixteen steps, the whole page. Written
lengths and rests only — exactly the holds vocabulary L3 installed, so
nothing here needs a primitive the player hasn't used. One of the four
candidates is the threes figure from the L3 board, so a quarter of the
rung is already half-known.

**Closer (7).** Sixteen steps, five notes, three lengths. Small
vocabulary on both axes: the difficulty is holding it, not decoding it.

## Retries and reveals

Every rung carries three or four candidate calls so a repeat attempt
draws a fresh one and the answer cannot be memorised. Which call is
drawn, how many tries a rung allows, and whether a miss reveals the
answer are **engine policy** — `briefs/echo.md`, not here. The copy
below assumes only that a rung can be failed and restarted.

## Quest copy drafts

*Per `QUEST-COPY.md`: constraints not goals, one rail-line per bullet,
the concept name lives only in* Teaches, *no absolute note names. The
seed line names the key as the existing boards do.*

### ⚔ Which Way *(seed: C major, 88)*
- two notes, played hidden
- answer the shape, not the notes
- up or down, nothing else
*Teaches: the ear's first question.*

### ⚔ Three Ways *(seed: G major, 92)*
- three notes, played hidden
- answer the shape only
- a note repeated is still a move
*Teaches: standing still is a direction.*

### ⚔ The Long Way *(seed: D minor, 96)*
- five or six notes, played hidden
- answer the shape only
- one wrong move, start over
*Teaches: a line held as a drawing.*

### ⚔ Home and Back *(seed: F major, 96)*
- three notes, played hidden
- answer the notes themselves
- every length the same
*Teaches: five notes go a long way.*

### ⚔ The Pull *(seed: C major, 92)*
- four notes, played hidden
- answer the notes themselves
- every length the same
*Teaches: the seventh has an errand.*

### ⚔ One Note Drum *(seed: A minor, 100)*
- sixteen steps, played hidden
- one pitch the whole way
- answer the lengths and the gaps
*Teaches: rhythm is a second ear.*

### ⚔ The Sentence *(seed: G major, 92)*
- sixteen steps, played hidden
- answer the notes and their lengths
- write it after it stops, not during
*Teaches: audiation.*

**Note on the last bullet of The Sentence:** it is a self-administered
constraint, the same class as the ten-loop test — the grid cannot check
whether the player wrote while the call was sounding. It is the only
bullet on the board that is not machine-checkable, and it is the one
that makes the rung the rung.

## Appendix — 4.2 shortlist

*4.2 is real music by ear at the grid. The capstone is already authored
(⚔ The Apprentice's Hand, `QUESTS.md`) and is not duplicated here; the
capstone line is the player's own pick and* To Zanarkand *right hand is
the finish line the curriculum names. What follows is warm-up material
for the rungs before it: short, singable, mostly stepwise lines that fit
eight to sixteen steps. Titles only —* **transcribing them is the
lesson**, *so nothing here is written out, and nothing here should be.*

1. **Victory Fanfare** (Final Fantasy series) — the shortest real line in
   the catalogue: a handful of notes, and most of the difficulty is the
   repeated-pitch rhythm, so it splits pitch from rhythm the way the 4.1
   ladder did.
2. **Chocobo Theme** (Final Fantasy series) — eight steps, small moves,
   known end to end without effort; the hum is already there to write
   down.
3. **Dearly Beloved** (Kingdom Hearts) — slow enough to audiate in real
   time, tiny range, almost entirely stepwise; the gentlest first
   dictation on the list.
4. **Aerith's Theme** (Final Fantasy VII) — a stepwise descent that hangs
   on one long note, which is exactly the holds vocabulary L3 built.
5. **Melodies of Life** (Final Fantasy IX) — written to be sung, so the
   phrase lengths are breath-sized, and its cadence leans home the way
   rung 5 did.
6. **Terra's Theme** (Final Fantasy VI) — an arch with two real leaps in
   it; worth doing contour-first before touching the pitches, which is
   the ladder's whole argument tested on real material.
7. **Lost Woods / Saria's Song** (The Legend of Zelda) — the curriculum's
   own reference for echo-play. Eight notes, one key, and the moves are
   leaps rather than steps, so it fails gracefully into contour work.
8. **Korobeiniki** (the Tetris line) — not Square, included as a control:
   near-purely stepwise, minor, and known cold, so a miss on it is a
   listening miss and nothing else.

Order is roughly easiest first. Five of the eight would be enough; the
list is longer than the lesson needs so the player can refuse two of them
without the board thinning out.
